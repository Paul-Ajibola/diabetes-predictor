Diabetes Progression Predictor

Understand the ten physiological inputs and how they can inform a progression prediction.

These definitions explain what each input represents and its potential predictive context. They do not describe a model-specific weight, a causal effect, or an individual medical assessment.

What is being predicted?

The Scikit-learn Diabetes reference dataset describes 442 people with diabetes, ten baseline inputs, and a quantitative measure of disease progression one year after baseline. These variable names match that dataset, but this does not confirm which dataset or model this application uses.

A progression score is not the probability of developing diabetes.

01 · Reading standardized values

Scaled inputs, not raw readings

Fields expect the same preprocessing as during training, not raw lab values.

The scikit-learn reference scaling

Columns are mean-centered and divided by the standard deviation, making every column zero-mean and unit-variance.

02 · Patient characteristics

Variable What it means Potential predictive context
ageAge at baseline, before the follow-up period.Adds demographic context. Does not determine progression on its own.
sexDataset-coded sex. Use the encoding specified by the training dataset.May capture group-level patterns. Do not infer causality.
bmiWeight relative to height, measured in kg/m² before scaling.A proxy for body size and adiposity that adds metabolic context.
bpAverage blood pressure at baseline.Adds cardiovascular context alongside body size and serum measurements.

03 · Serum measurements

Variable What it means Potential predictive context
s1 · TCA measure of total cholesterol found in the blood.Provides overall lipid context.
s2 · LDLLipoproteins that carry cholesterol, often referred to as "bad" cholesterol.Adds atherogenic lipid context.
s3 · HDLLipoproteins involved in cholesterol transport, often referred to as "good" cholesterol.Adds complementary lipid context.
s4 · TCHA calculated ratio comparing total cholesterol to high-density lipoproteins.Summarizes the balance between total cholesterol and HDL.
s5 · LTGLikely the natural logarithm of serum triglycerides levels.Adds metabolic and lipid context.
s6 · GLUA measure of serum glucose levels.Adds glycemic context to the joint prediction.

04 · How the variables work together

A trained regression model learns joint statistical relationships between the baseline inputs and the progression target. It uses the combined pattern of demographic, body size, cardiovascular, lipid, and glycemic information to estimate a score—not ten independent medical judgments.

05 · What makes a prediction plausible?

Match the training inputs

Use the same preprocessing, variable order, and sex encoding as training.

Stay within represented data

Use valid, internally consistent inputs within the population and ranges represented in training.

Evaluate the fitted model

Assess performance on held-out data, not the training data alone.

Educational reference, not medical advice

A progression prediction is not a diagnosis, a probability of developing diabetes, or treatment advice. This guide is not patient-specific medical advice and does not replace assessment by a qualified clinician.
